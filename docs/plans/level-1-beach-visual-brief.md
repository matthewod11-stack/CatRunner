# Level 1 Beach Visual Brief

Final Phase 1 art direction for `BEACH` from archived `ROADMAP_V4.md`.

## Direction

Sunny Shore should read as a bright arcade beach runner: clean silhouettes, bold outlines, warm sand, cyan water, and readable foreground hazards. The level should feel playful and energetic without turning the playfield into visual noise.

## Palette

| Role | Colors | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sky | `#7fd7ff`, `#ffe7a1` | full-canvas gradient, light enough to keep airborne threats readable |
| Ocean | `#1fb7d7`, `#0e7ea0`, `#f7ffff` | midground tile and foam, secondary to the sand play lane |
| Sand | `#f5c96b`, `#eab85d`, `#c58f3c` | ground and Sand Monster family |
| Hazard accents | `#ef4444`, `#f97316`, `#fde047` | crabs, beachball, hit/fight states |
| Power-up accents | `#3b82f6`, `#8b5cf6`, `#22c55e` | speed, magnet, super-size pickups |
| Ink | `#273043`, `#5b4b3d` | consistent readable outline and interior detail |

## Line Style

- Chunky vector arcade shapes with consistent dark outline.
- Avoid painterly texture at gameplay scale.
- Interior lines should clarify state or object identity, not add decoration.
- Hazards and pickups need stronger edge contrast than background entities.

## Contrast And Readability

- Foreground gameplay elements use true-alpha art and dark outlines.
- Background entities stay lower alpha in runtime and should not compete with hazards.
- Seagulls, projectiles, shells, and power-ups must be recognizable at their current configured display sizes.
- Shell ammo and score coins must remain visually distinct.

## Horizon And Parallax

- Sky fills the canvas.
- Ocean sits in the midground band above the sand.
- Foam marks the boundary between water and ground.
- Sand owns the bottom play lane.
- Distant objects move slowly and stay secondary; chaos entities can be more saturated but still sit behind gameplay.

## UI Relationship

- React-owned HUD remains the primary information surface.
- Phaser art should leave top HUD space readable and avoid placing busy high-contrast background details behind HUD elements.
- Boss alert, shell throw hint, hit feedback, and particles should reinforce gameplay state without becoming the only readable signal.

## Current Phase 1 Asset Approach

The Phase 1 pack is authored as committed SVG source generated by `scripts/generate-beach-art-pack.mjs` under `assets/sprites/beach/`.

This gives the project:

- true transparent vector gameplay assets
- deterministic committed source files
- a level-local directory layout matching the Phase 0 target shape
- a quick baseline that can be iterated during runtime QA without returning to ad hoc PNG swaps

The first in-browser review pass tightened the pack around player readability:

- sun and clouds were simplified so the sky reads cleanly instead of noisy
- boats were made larger and higher contrast while staying secondary
- the beachball was reshaped with curved panels and spherical shading
- the sandcastle was scaled up for a stronger obstacle read
- planes now enter left-to-right to create depth against right-to-left boats
- Sand Monster states were upgraded with stronger expression, texture, and hit/defeat reads

## Acceptance Notes

- Final art may still be tuned during Phase 4 playtest, but all world-art families now have deliberate assets.
- The static gameplay cat remains out of Phase 1 scope; the animated runner sheet is Phase 2.
- The Beach pack should be evaluated in-game before applying this approach to future levels.
